Randomiser pages

  1. R1Fruit Machine
    Three reels - Flavour, Game type, Hook - with locks, image slots and the one-line pitch. Functional first pass; the looks come next.    Open →
  2. R2Quiet Power - Blue + Indigo
    The same three reels in Concept 8's restrained dark language: three flat tiles, one drum each, the reel's state in a pill, and the pitch in two lines - game type and flavour, then the hook. Blue spins, Indigo holds.    Open →
  3. R2bQuiet Power - One Widget
    Randomiser 2's colours, moulded: the machine drawn as a device - a case with a lit recessed window and one sheet of glass across three reels that sit as close together as the glass allows. The hold key under each reel is the same case-grey key as the unlock button at rest, turns into the library's yellow-400 and sinks into the case while it is holding, and is yellow-200 under the pointer in either state - a lamp, on the ramp. The spin and unlock keys sit in a rail down the right-hand side; all of the depth is shadows and highlights, and the colours and the type are still this page's own.    Open →
  4. R2cQuiet Power - Portrait Reels
    2b's machine with a reel entry turned upright: a bigger picture with its name underneath, the reel's size in brackets beside its name instead of in a head of its own, no panel head and no status line. Taller entries and narrower reels mean the widget shrinks to 880px and still fits three across a phone. The reels turn the moment the page opens, and the machine has a voice - a tick per entry and a clack as each reel lands, with one small key to silence it.    Open →
  5. R3Broadcast Scoreboard

  7. R5Terminal

Question pages

  1. Q1Default
    The current design: Step 1 picks a category from ten cards, Step 2 the exact game type, read from the app's data. The baseline to compare new designs against.    Open →
  2. Q2Quiet Power - Blue + Indigo
    The same screen in Concept 8b's language: one hairline for every boundary, a blue-to-indigo progress rail, the chosen answer lit in Blue with the indigo edge highlight off its ready card, the draft tag in Indigo. The reference design - the dark styling the three material studies below move away from. Q6 to Q9 are the on-brand options: four structures inside Epic's own system, for choosing between.    Open →
  3. Q3Cassette Deck
    First step away from dark, and a change of material: the page is a 90s hi-fi unit. Warm grey plastic with real bevels, a printed face plate and serial number, dark LCD windows with amber numerals for the step and the counter, a level meter for progress, and answers that latch down like a source selector, each with its own lamp. Progress is a counter, not a bar.    Open →
  4. Q4Blueprint
    Further out again: a technical drawing on light drafting stock. Graph grid, blue ink, no fills at all, dimension lines with an arrowhead at each end, an item schedule with spec numbers, a calibration bar carrying the progress and a title block in the bottom-right corner. The recorded direction called for the deep blue sheet; this one is drawn on white stock and quotes the deep blue once, in the title block, because this slot exists to leave the dark behind.    Open →
  5. Q5Riso Zine
    The furthest from dark, and the only one of the nine that is not a screen: a sheet printed by hand. Off-white stock with a visible grain, two inks overprinting a third colour, everything misregistered by a millimetre, answers as cut-paper strips held on with tape, an ink bar for progress, a folio number on the question and a hand-numbered edition line at the foot.    Open →
  6. Q6Launcher
    The first of four on-brand options, built on measured values from launcher.epicgames.com rather than on a reading of the brand: its canvas #0E0B21, one large purple bloom at the 70% opacity the page publishes, Inter Tight 700 at -0.035em, a 20px/400 standfirst at 65% white, the [01] numbered step, and the launcher's own call to action - #33BFFF with a #0A2633 label in a 48px, 10px-radius pill. The chosen card takes Epic's own select tokens: the #28282C elevated-high-select fill, a #26BBFF border and a Selected chip.    Open →
  7. Q7Store
    The same question laid out as a store listing: a title instead of a hero, a filter row carrying the count and the draft filter, and a two-up product grid whose cards say something useful in their metadata row - how many game types a category holds, or an option's state in the app's own words, in Epic's status colours (Researched green, Draft amber, basics neutral). Each card wears one of Epic's three published 225deg card gradients as placeholder key art, and the marking ring on its bottom-right fills with the select blue and a tick.    Open →
  8. Q8Guide
    The least landing page and the most like a screen inside the app: a guide rail on the left naming the whole journey - Category, Game type, Devices, Settings, Build plan, which are the four things the data already stores per game type - with the current step marked and the progress reported under the steps, and the answers as a list of rows with a radio at the head of each. Choosing one fills the row and the radio; nothing moves.    Open →
  9. Q9Console
    The question as a tool: the engine site's #0B0B0B canvas, a breadcrumb and a monospace read-out where a hero would be, and the answers as rows of a table with a monospace index down the left and a state chip on the right. Epic's status semantics do the work - Researched, Draft, Basics only - so the reason an option is or is not available is stated on the row rather than hidden behind a toggle, and Dev mode is a real checkbox with the engine site's #26BBFF fill and #101014 tick.    Open →
